# What worked

- The intro - it had its own story to tell, and sets the events up very well. It managed to show both opinions on the subject of death penalty, a small story of an honest man who made a mistake (he still killed a student) and the system decided that death penalty was the only viable punishment - which raises moral questions, and also showed how corrupt and morally bankrupt the jail system can be, which sets the stage for all events that follow.
- The Rashomon effect where Pasupathy’s character who presents events mostly in their true form, but twists certain very specific facts in his favor. The multiple camera angles in the two versions of the story really emphasize this, and it was lovely to watch
- The subtle details and callbacks were amazing, so much so that it could be timeless. Virumaandi getting help from every single person he wronged in the past along with the bull, Annalakshmi willingly hanging herself to death which is called back to in the final scene, Virumaandi using the lever mechanism which opens the floor and suspends death penalty convicts on the noose, in an action choreography to save the lives of two journalists, were all notable examples.
- The entire third act could have been a separate movie, but they managed to fit in a lot of action, gore, and emotions that it was engaging until the very end.

# What didn’t work

- Kamal Hasan’s intro was so dragged out, and the screenplay failed to setup the stakes for his entry in an engaging manner. The stakes are still there, but the entire portion of setting up the jallikattu, Virumaandi’s entry, and him subduing the bull was stretched out a bit too much.
- Too much horny. We get it, you love your wife. But, there was only one scene where the horny was required for the plot, but instead, there were like 4 or 5.
