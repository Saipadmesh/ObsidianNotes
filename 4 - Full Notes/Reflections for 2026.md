Today I saw a video by the content creator "Epaphra", who was describing how he earned his first crore as a content creator and agency business owner. While working in a company, he apparently used to go to office early and spend his free time learning skills. He only decided to start aiming to be a content creator when he realized he already had an existing set of skills which aligned with that job. That job got him credibility as a storyteller, and brands started requesting him to make content. This led to him building an agency of 15 people who handle content creation and marketing for startups. He still calls himself an "accidental businessman", because his first aim started with content creation, but one thing led to another, and he is rich now.

There are learnings which I can gain from this story. I have spent two years in Bangalore experimenting with multiple hobbies and passions, but almost all of them were inspired by external factors. Both acting and direction needed external motivation for me to get into, instead of me willingly choosing them as hobbies I intend to follow. And that's okay, I am forever grateful to the people who nudged me in the right direction, and I am grateful for myself for picking on the right cues and expanding in a way that has truly led to me living two years with fulfillment. But it is high time for me to not just choose what skills I want to focus on, but first reflect on my existing knowledge, see at what instances I felt excited the most, and try to build experiments I can follow for a year, because committing to a life-long goal scares me.

I need to look back on my past two years worth of work, and the events that happened that have led to my growth, which happened intentionally or unintentionally. 

When I first joined my job in 2023, I was excited to work and put in my best efforts. And I tried hard to get my manager's approval and to fit into the corporate culture. But I also had this intent of not wanting to commit myself to only my job. Even before theatre or direction, I was trying to workout, and make music on my own in the PG. As time had progressed, I think I have come to the conclusion that I can't lead a life which is restricted to a corporate job and corporate job only. I need to have goals on the side which do not require optimization or provide immediate value. But I do recognize the impact this mindset has caused in my work, because I feel like I am currently unable to provide reasonable value for the salary that I'm getting. 

I joined misfits in 2024 officially. I participated in two workshops which taught me the basics of acting, but more importantly, taught me so much about my own body and mind. I may not be a great actor now, but those workshops have helped give me the ability and confidence to try things and fail without my ego getting in the way. And that, I feel, is the most important lesson one can learn in their lives. I think the workshops also made me understand the lack of control I have over my own body, which I feel is still an opportunity for me to grow. Along with understanding myself, I also understood the need for a community in every person's life. There were so many different shades of people, who had gone through so varied life experiences all coming together to interact and enact characters all made up in their heads. And that was so beautiful.

In 2025, I wrote, directed and produced a short film titled "Oh Shit!". Though the opportunity to direct one came out of external circumstances, something inside me deeply wanted to make a film, no matter what. I didn't know what I wanted to make, I didn't know how to even begin, but I just had to do it. In February, Sateesh came to me with the opportunity to direct a skit. Gomathi extended the script, and we had actors ready, but it didn't go as planned, because it was too big of a project to take on. I took a break, and came back with another script, a simple one, with just two actors. There were eight people on the day of the shoot. The two actors - Sateesh and Aswin, the DOP Rahul Ravindra and his assistant Mohan, Nishanth, Raj, and Aayush. Then during the dubbing process, I met Joshua and Prajwal, who contributed to the sound design and mixing of the film. I made my own music and edited the entire short film. Throughout the length of this project, there were so many fights and difficult conversations which led to a lot of delay and mental fatigue for me personally. Despite this, I am really grateful for each and every one of them. Being put in the spot as the director was really enjoyable, but I noticed my lack of quick decision making to be a problem throughout the process, which now not just affected me, but all the people involved. I have always been very risk averse, because I tend to be very anxious before taking a decision, but the truth is, I don't want to be this way. I don't want to accept that I am a slow, calculative mover in life. I want to be someone who can take decisions and stick with them, while knowing when to re-align and rethink. 

Other than these major milestones, I have also experimented with the gym, calisthenics, and boxing for my physical health, though I have not been properly consistent with any of them. I fell into a research hole when I was going to the gym, so theoretically I know the best ways to build muscle, but I was inconsistent, and every time I did try, my mind naturally diverted into feeling bad about my looks, which really took away from my confidence. I believe that even in terms of health, I need to learn something more skill based, which helps my health, so that I can focus on getting better at the skill, rather than my body. Boxing is one thing I really enjoyed in that regard. I joined a class near my home in August 2025. Learning to fight has always felt very relevant to me, maybe because I grew up in a sterile environment, or maybe because I was bullied in school. To experience the joy of failing and getting back up to defeat an opponent (defeat, not break) is something I still yearn for. But once again, I grew inconsistent, and eventually, the trainer shifted to a new place, which is inaccessible to me without a vehicle. I want to experience controlled competition and mastery over my own body and make it move and adapt quickly, and boxing is the best way for that with whatever I currently know. Calisthenics for me is a quick band-aid solution, but a it is really really good one. It is very accessible, meaning there's no friction for me to start, and it is a quick workout that takes hardly 45 minutes. Not a lot of planning, just brainless physical work (ref: [[Simpler Workouts, Happier Lifestyle]]). And it feels good that I have progressed from doing wall push ups to ten proper pushups. It is a great way for me to learn to control my body, but it's just not as cool as boxing and I have to motivate myself constantly because there's nobody watching. In general, all this has taught me how important it is for my body to keep moving and keep building, no matter which way I do it. I owe it to my body to feel good. I owe it to myself to build a body which can function for several years.

Now, with all this information and learnings from the last three years, I am stepping into 2026. I have lost touch with almost everyone I worked with in the past outside of my job. I am starting from scratch, and I want to take on new experiments. My one main success in these two years is that I never gave up. I had the audacity to try new things and finish them. If it is a project with an end goal, then there is nothing that will stop me from reaching there, no matter how long it takes, or how difficult it is. I also take pride in constantly trying to learn more about myself and the world I live in, instead of accepting opinions as truths or being fully content with where I am to a point where it leads to inaction. My failures, or in corporate language, "opportunities" are in the areas of decision making and actually selecting and sticking to a goal in the long term. I need to learn to trust myself yet again, to take decisions which benefit my body, mind and soul, and to master skills which are important to me to lead a fulfilling life.

I also need to ensure I don't over commit in my decisions, and cut down to a point where all my core needs are met week by week, while leaving remaining time be free for me to experiment with random things for a holistic growth. If I take all of my goals equally as serious, then my stress and [[Allostatic Load]] increase, until I get burnt out and equally pull away from all my goals.
