I have this cycle that I've noticed constantly repeats, ever since I moved to Bangalore. It starts with a lack of motivation to do the things I want to do, followed by that one project that excites me enough to break me out from my static state, for which I end up obsessing over. If the project succeeds, it's a huge dopamine rush, and if it doesn't, it leads to burnout. Either of those routes brings me back to my rest state and the cycle repeats again. I assumed that this cycle only exists because I might be having ADHD and my dopamine receptors are messed up. And maybe it is still the case.

Very recently I stumbled upon a video talking about this "allostatic load". Allostasis is a mechanism where repeated exposure to a new environment helps the body physiologically adapt to it. Hence allostatic load refers to the unnecessary mental and physical toll forced on the body through repeated exposure to stressful environments. In this day and age, that's basically just another Monday. I wake up and scroll reels for half an hour. Then I skip shower and sit on the computer for my work. In the middle of it, I turn on my other laptop to watch youtube videos. Oh, and I use the phone while I use the restroom, and just before going to sleep. Basically all these years of constantly exposing myself to consuming content, and by letting my imagination run free for it to create entire storylines just for me to get anxious, I have put repeated stress on my brain, which causes it to lose focus, and burnout quite often. Maybe my dopamine receptors are messed up, but my porn usage alone isn't the sole reason for that (though it most definitely is one of the many)

So what is the solution for this? Recovery, not rest. When I come back from work, and immediately start playing Hades 2, my body may be resting, but my mind is still being used. The brain is a muscle and I've already trained it to failure by then. Sure resting helps heal muscles, but there's no faster healing than when you do active recovery stretches. The brain requires similar recovery mechanisms. The suggested ones are taking a hot/cold shower, meditation, exercise, massage and basically anything that changes your heart rate drastically. The drastic change helps regulate the heart, so that it slows down and your body can calm down.

It sounds like a bunch of bullshit, but it made sense for me. It made sense that I'm constantly under stress while also procrastinating. So I decided to try fixing it. A nice hot shower for a long duration, a quick breathing exercise, and also, regular exercise. I also reduced my Youtube and Instagram time. And I think I was able to feel several times when my brain felt overloaded. I think this could be an important concept in psychology, but I've heard therapists ask people to name their emotions when they're going through something intense and turbulent. Apparently it helps to regulate the emotion. Maybe that's what that video did to me. It gave me a name to something that I've probably felt for years. And it helped.
