I have lived in Coimbatore for a very long time, before I moved to Bangalore. Though I started learning about political discrimination and its implications during as early as college, it took me some time to recognize and label certain events as discriminatory. I want to lay down two such events that happened today, which revolve around this theme, because, like that Rick and Morty joke, it is weird that two similar events happened on the exact same day.

## Event 1

In the morning, as I was having my breakfast, my dad who is sitting nearby, plays a voice recording forwarded in our colony association's WhatsApp group aloud, which prompts me to listen. In the recording, the colony president says that a few people representing the local government came to his house the previous day, and requested him to lend our community hall for 11 days in the mornings, in order to serve food to the garbage collectors across multiple colonies. The president had "strictly" denied permission saying that the community hall is built by and for our community, and not for the government. His additional justification was that, there is a school area which is already allocated to the government by the colony, so they should handle this there. But the representatives had some excuse for it. In the voice recording, the president urged all colony members to show up at his house, because a higher official from the government was planning to come there to request further in the near future.

On hearing this, my dad was entirely on the president's side. "Why here, when there is already an allocated space?" was his question. My question was simpler - why not? Why not serve the people who serve you everyday? Why not do a good deed and serve people who are part of the system that you benefit out of? "Because they'll make the entire place unclean" was my parents' response. So garbage collectors, whose entire job is to transport the filth from your houses every single day, will make the hall unclean? Why not just ensure there are proper garbage disposal exists for used plates and wasted food? The colony doesn't bear the costs for the food or transport, all the officials are asking for is a place for serving. And the funniest thing about that whole incident was how the colony president, in the voice recording, tried to emotionally blackmail the members into joining the "cause", by saying "If you truly care for the colony, come". It's as if the privileged were forming a group to revolt against the oppressed.

## Event 2

We have a maid who comes to our house everyday except Sunday. She practically helped raise me, that's how long she's been working here, and that's how close she is to our family. Today morning, my mom and I saw her wonkily walking on the road as she was trying to reach our house. When asked whether she was fine, she showed her ankles which were both swollen. I immediately told my mom to wait on the side of the road so that I can quickly go drop her to our house and come back. My mom's initial reaction was "She will go on her own, it's anyway close by now". I'm glad I didn't listen to her.

Later in the afternoon, I offered to drop her to her house as well. My mom, unwilling to go because she has leg pain herself, told me to drop her off until the end of a road, because beyond that point, it'll be full of garbage and spit, and the maid can walk herself. Honestly, that felt like such a fake reason, that I decided to not listen to her once again. As I rode the scooter beyond that specific point, even our maid nudged softly for me to stop, because she could walk. I ignored that and decided to drop her at the entrance of her house. Along the way, her neighbor was smiling at her, because someone had come in a vehicle to drop her off. As she got down, she offered me to come to her house but since I had work, I had to refuse. Her house owner was nearby and she pointed in his direction, and when I looked at him, this 65 year old man raises and folds his hands to me. It felt awkward but I repeated the same gesture and came back. On one hand, people looking at me in surprise felt good, but on the other hand, the only reason they did that was because I was an unfamiliar rich boy in their neighbourhood. 

## Final Thoughts

It is interesting how in both of these events, my parents have this internalized sense of caste pride, which I have usually failed to notice because in the past, they made it a point to never teach me about caste. Maybe they recognized their prejudices but could not find a way to stop them, and decided that the least they can do is to make sure that it doesn't affect their next generation. Or maybe they just didn't really care to explain all of this to me and have kept it to themselves, and the only times that this prejudice comes up is when their identity is threatened. Either way, I'm grateful. I'm grateful that I wasn't taught whether my caste is Nair or Menon. I'm grateful to not have a caste name attached to mine. I'm grateful that I have never been part of groups which try to inculcate and normalize caste based discrimination. And I'm especially grateful for my parents for allowing me to voice my opinions, even though it may have questioned their ego and gone against their beliefs.
