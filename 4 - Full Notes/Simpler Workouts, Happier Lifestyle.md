Gym bros love to hear the word "volume". Not the one that you control on your speakers, but rather the ones that tire out your body. And it makes sense. To get the most out of your workout and build your boulder shoulders, you need to absolutely destroy those muscles, and give yourself enough rest so that your body heals and grows in size. I have been working out, or at least trying to work out, for the past two years, and even though my biceps aren't shaped like mountain peaks and my forearms look as thin as my calves, I have spent a lot of time researching the most optimal workout routines and training splits to follow. Many science based lifters, like Jeff Nippard agree that every muscle group can be targeted twice, with around 8-10 sets per week per muscle group for the most optimal growth. What nobody mentioned though, was the fact that you need spend half of your life within the gym if you need to properly get those sets in. And there's also this problem of physically going back to the gym after all the rest days. That honestly was the hardest part for me, even though rest days are practically essential to build muscle, and are not optional.

Out of the countless videos I've seen, I don't remember which one helped me get over this perfectionist mindset, but this year, I believe that I have an objectively better workout plan which is specifically customized for my schedule and interests, instead of solely focusing on a single goal. I always have this fascination for doing and being good at multiple things at once, and specialization actually feels boring. I only realized recently that I can apply the same fascination to my workouts to make them more fun and interesting, while working towards better overall health, more than building a body like a tank. The plan involves three days of calisthenics, three days of zone 2 cardio, and one day of shadow boxing.

Switching from bodybuilding to calisthenics might probably be the best change I have done from last year, because the goal shifts from building the maximum amount of muscle in the shortest period of time, to getting really good at the skill of balancing your body in various positions. The muscle building becomes a side effect more than the sole purpose. And since I'm pretty weak to do some of the harder progressions, I only need to focus on the absolute basics, that is pushups, pullups and squats, for now. I even don't need to worry about cutting or bulking, because the goal is still to get better at the skill. Even eating at maintenance while increasing my protein intake would be more than enough to gain the strength for these foundational movements. All of this takes off so much of the mental load, and should hopefully help me improve my consistency.

One other thing I didn't like about gym was the lack of emphasis on cardio. I mean, what's the point in having a Porsche if the engine belongs to Maruti?
