Until the second year of college, I used to play this little niche videogame called Skyrim. It is an open world RPG set in a fantasy world and all that, but what made it so replayable was the ability to mod the game to one's liking. The game was released in 2011, but mods and the modding community have kept it alive ever since. The game is probably still gonna be active until the world ends. Among the mods were those that improve gameplay to modern standards, and those that improve the graphics. I had a potato laptop which could barely run the unmodded version of the game in high settings. But being the ambitious prick that I am, I decided to put my laptop through hell by downloading these mods that improve the game's visuals and pray to God that my laptop doesn't burst in flames.

The great thing about these visual mods was that it was very customizable, allowing you to find the right balance between visuals and performance. You could change the resolution, switch off anti aliasing, or turn down the reflection and shadow quality because who wants to reflect on their actions in a videogame anyway. But among those settings was this one specific setting which was such a pain in the ass, called God Rays. First of all, the name itself sounds pretentiously Christian. And I didn't even know what the effect does for a good amount of time, just knew that the moment I turn it on, the game becomes a stop motion video for me. Eventually I did figure out what it does. Whenever the sun is out, and you look up at it in game, god rays changes the visuals so that there are distinct streaks of light falling on the character, which makes it very aesthetic to look at, especially when you're around foliage, because the light spills through the gaps among the trees. This small but not so subtle effect made the in game world feel like heaven, but that's probably where my laptop would be right now if I hadn't turned it off.

And of course, no wonder this effect is so resource consuming, because the easiest way to simulate this would be to keep track of all light sources currently on screen, along with the obstacles that can block light partially and the areas in which light should be rendered as visible rays - because in nature this effect only happens in foggy and misty areas with several floating particles that scatter light, and physically render ray samples during runtime. All of these samples and their vectors are stored and processed in the GPU. Now that I think about it, a GPU is literally just an advanced calculator, but for rendering pretty pictures. There are simpler ways as well to simulate god rays like 2D post processing, but they might not be entirely accurate. But hey, isn't all of gaming just smoke and mirrors?

Today I woke up early in the morning and went for a walk around a nearby lake. It was foggy and cold and I was half asleep, trying to wake up as much as I was trying to walk. I came across a path which was covered with trees on the side. I looked up to yawn but my tiredness instantly turned to awe because, god rays. The trees hid the sun perfectly but the light spilled through the gaps between the leaves and branches to form these divine rays which hit my face, like a blessing from heaven. At that moment, I felt, maybe this phenomenon did indeed deserve the name "God Rays". I was experiencing an inexplicable work of art in real time, even though the scientific reasoning behind the phenomenon could be explained by a ten year old. I took a deep breath to relax, only to realize that the particles that helped the light form its shape was coming from all the dust and pollution from the nearby traffic.
