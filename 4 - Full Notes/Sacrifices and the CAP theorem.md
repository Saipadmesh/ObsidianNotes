When designing the delivery system of a production level application, there are three pillars to consider - Consistency, Availability and Partition Tolerance.
- Consistency refers to the how quickly the changes present in one node (or server or pod) propagate to all the other nodes in the system. An example of high consistency would be multiple people present in a group chat receiving the latest message at the same time. An example of low consistency would be that government PF website which cannot figure out that you just changed your password and refreshed the page.
- Availability refers to how responsive the system is at any given time to the end user. It is measured in percentage. An app that runs purely on your phone without any internet is probably the highest availability there is, and once again, that fucking PF website honestly has a 5% availability, because it just kept chucking me out to the home page.
- Partition tolerance is just ensuring that the system functions even if one node in the system fails. Those fairy lights have zero partition tolerance because if one bulb goes out, the entire length of the wire stops working. Parallel lights probably have good tolerance. But in complex computer architecture, this probably means that there should be multiple DB instances and multiple servers, and if one DB instance fails, the system can rely on the other instances without shutting down.

Of course, these are just the basics, but what's interesting to me about all this is that, the CAP theorem states that you can never aim for all three of these at the same time, meaning you can only aim for two of the three at max, and then hope for the best that the third one is also covered.

If you aim for consistency and partition tolerance, your system is going to ensure that any user present anywhere around the world is going to see the same results, and the chances of two people seeing two different results for the same query is highly unlikely. But this is at the cost of some of those users being unlucky and the system becoming temporarily unavailable to them to access. A good example for this could be banking systems.

If you aim for consistency and availability, it sounds like a great start for an e-commerce platform. But high consistency and availability also means that your system could be very tightly coupled or could have so many different nodes all over the place to ensure availability, which means that ensuring a proper partition tolerance is going to be very difficult. It looks like only a monolithic architecture with minimal user base can aim for consistency and availability.

If you aim for availability and partition tolerance, you want your system to be always available to any number of end users at the cost of showing them slightly older data sometimes. All social media websites aim for this, because they so badly wanna get you hooked and make you stay there, instead of moving to another website, or god forbid, close your phone and sleep.

What's so interesting to me about this is that, there is no perfect solution to this. Like the top CEO's with their trillion dollar salaries still can't really have all three of these, even though that would be the most ideal solution for any system. They just pick one of the three (technically two) options, sacrifice one of the pillars, and roll with it. Maybe all of life is like that. Maybe you just need to pick your struggle, move ahead with it, and hope you made the right choice, instead of trying to experience everything everywhere all at once.
